# Working notes — how big is the model horse hobby?

**Researched 2026-08-21.** Working notes for
[`../BUSINESS_MODEL_2026.md`](../BUSINESS_MODEL_2026.md).

> **The honest headline: this hobby publishes almost no usable statistics.** Facebook, where
> most of the hobby actually lives, is behind a login wall. Both marketplace domains
> (`modelhorsesalespages.com`, `modelhorseconnection.com`) currently have **expired TLS
> certificates and would not resolve**, so active-listing counts — the single best commercial
> signal — could not be obtained. NAMHSA has published no show list since 2019-20. Everything
> below is built around the few hard numbers that do exist, and every estimate is flagged.

---

## 1. The best single anchor: 13,000

Not attendance — **manufacturing volume**. The BreyerFest Celebration Model is included with
every VIP, All-Access and Online Traditional Access ticket, and Breyer makes almost exactly
as many as there are ticket-holders.

| Year | Model | Edition size | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2024 | Athenian Lady | **13,000** | https://identifymybreyer.com/Events/BreyerFest/breyerfest2024.html |
| 2025 | Tight Lines | **13,000** | https://www.breyerhorses.com/blogs/breyerfest-blog/breyerfest-limited-editions |

**Confidence: high** for 2024 (primary collector database), medium for 2025. Two consecutive
years at an identical figure reads as a deliberate production ceiling. Discounting
multi-ticket households and retail overstock: **~9,000–13,000 unique committed
participants.** This is a far more honest number than the attendance headline, because
someone bought a ticket.

Corroborating signals on the committed core:
- **Breyer Web Specials run 750–1,000 pieces**, one per person, Collector-Club-only, with a
  standby list — Breyer's own read on its most committed cohort.
- BreyerFest special runs typically 2,000–2,500 pieces.

## 2. BreyerFest attendance — softer than it looks

- **~30,000 attendees**, 2025 — https://www.morganhorse.com/home/index/detail/11605/
- "Upward of 35,000" combining in-person **and virtual**, 2025 — same source cluster
- **35,000 fans**, 2023, virtual attendees from 100+ countries — https://eqliving.com/breyerfest-2023/
- Grew from ~1,000 in 1990

**Confidence: medium-LOW as a measure of people.** These are almost certainly person-days
across a three-day event, blended with virtual participants, and include accompanying
children and casual horse-lovers. The figure has barely moved across 2023/2024/2025, which
reads like a recycled press-release number. **Do not treat 30,000 as 30,000 collectors.**

The repo's own July 2026 research (`docs/OPERATOR_PLAYBOOK.md`, `docs/STRATEGY_2026-07.md`)
recorded "BreyerFest ≈ 35,000 attendees" — consistent with the above, and subject to the same
caveat. The 13,000 ticket figure is the better anchor and supersedes it for modelling.

## 3. Breyer / Reeves International

- Private New Jersey corporation, founded 1946, acquired Breyer 1984 —
  https://www.breyerhorses.com/pages/about-reeves (**high confidence**)
- ~57–60 employees, Aug 2025 — https://growjo.com/company/Reeves_International (**medium**)
- Revenue estimates **conflict badly**: $35M / $15.6M / $14.3M, all third-party scrapers on a
  private company. **Do not cite a point estimate.**
- Breyer's own language is deliberately vague — collecting is "shared by thousands of horse
  lovers across the world." **No collector-base number is published anywhere.**

## 4. Social following — top of funnel, heavily diluted

| Channel | Figure | Confidence |
|---|---|---|
| Facebook page | **128,183 likes**, 2,352 "talking about this" | medium (search snippet) |
| Instagram `@breyermodelhorsesofficial` | **91,200 followers** (Aug 2026) | **high** (fetched live) |
| YouTube (Breyer Horse Network) | ~27,900 subscribers | medium |

The **1.8% "talking about this" ratio** on Facebook is the tell: these are brand-page likes
accumulated over 15+ years — parents, children, lapsed collectors — not an active hobbyist
count. Deduped across platforms, roughly **150,000–180,000 unique brand followers**.

## 5. Organized showing — this segment is CONTRACTING

NAN entrant and model counts, from https://namhsa.org/nan-archives/ (**high confidence —
primary organisation source**):

| Year | Entrants | Models |
|---|---|---|
| 2024 | **126** | 4,521 |
| 2022 | 101 | 3,409 |
| 2018 | 99 | 2,863 |
| 2016 | 140 | 3,655 |
| 2014 | 212 | 5,703 |
| 2008 | 226 | — |
| 2004 | **230** | — |

**Two findings that matter more than the totals.** First, **NAN is now biennial** — even
years only; 2017, 2019, 2020, 2021, 2023 and 2025 were skipped or social-only. Second,
**entrants are down ~45% since the mid-2000s (230 → 126) while models per entrant rose from
27 to 36.** Fewer people, each showing more horses. That is consolidation, not growth.

NAMHSA-sanctioned shows per year, counted from primary archive pages (**high confidence**):
**146** (2003-04) → **177** (2013-14) → **187** (2015-16). NAMHSA has published **no show list
since 2019-20**. An independent forward calendar lists **45 shows for Aug 2026 – Apr 2027**
across ~50 states and provinces (https://modelhorsego-n-show.weebly.com/live-shows.html), not
exhaustive. NAMHSA membership numbers are not published; $30 per two years is the only public
figure.

**`IMEHA`, formerly a major online showing organisation, shut down in 2018.** There has been
no incumbent in online showing since.

## 6. Forums, marketplaces, and one very sobering number

- **Model Horse Blab: 6,960 registered members, 182,769 threads, 3,429,661 posts** —
  https://modelhorseblab.com/ (**high confidence**, live stats block). But that is
  *cumulative registrations since ~2002*, and the site still displays "Most of Blab is
  restored, the rest is coming soon" — recovering from its multi-year outage, paid tiers
  suspended.
- **MH$P was hit by ransomware in March 2024** and rebranded to Model Horse Connection.
  **Both domains now serve expired certificates.** The hobby's default marketplace
  infrastructure is visibly fragile. (**medium-high**)
- **Star Dapple: 345 listings** — https://martha.net/2024/09/where-to-sell-model-horses-and-props/
- **Model Horse Social (`modelhorses.com`): 158 members** (**high confidence**, fetched live).

> ### The 158-member precedent
>
> `modelhorses.com` is a purpose-built standalone social platform for exactly this hobby, and
> it has **158 members**. This is the most important number in this file for planning
> purposes. It is not proof that a platform cannot work here — MHH is a far more capable
> product and Model Horse Social is a thin social layer — but it is hard evidence that
> *building it* and *them coming* are different projects, and that the competitor to beat is
> **free Facebook groups**, not other websites.

- BreyerHorseRef holds **9,000+ distinct Breyer models** — a catalog-depth signal, and
  comparable to MHH's own 10,500+ entry reference spine.

**Gaps that could not be closed:** Facebook group member counts (login wall; www, m. and
mbasic all attempted), r/ModelHorses subscribers (Reddit blocked by policy in this
environment), Discord server sizes (403), MH$P active listings (dead cert), NAMHSA
membership, eBay/Etsy listing volumes (403/timeout).

## 7. Synthesis — three layers

| Layer | Low | **Central** | High | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A — English-speaking people who collect at all** | 150,000 | **~350,000** | 900,000 | **Low** |
| **B — internet-active hobbyists** (groups, forums, showing, buying/selling online) | 30,000 | **~70,000** | 150,000 | **Low-medium** |
| **C — serious/engaged core plausibly reachable by a collection-management + showing + price-guide platform** | 15,000 | **~20,000** | 35,000 | **Low-medium** |

**How Layer A was derived.** Two triangulations. From the committed core: 13,000 BreyerFest
ticket-holders at a typical 2–5% core-to-casual ratio gives 260,000–650,000. From social
reach: ~150–180K deduped unique brand followers, which typically captures only a fraction of
casual collectors. Both point at a few hundred thousand. **This layer rests on ratio
assumptions, not measurement.**

**How Layer B was derived.** BreyerFest ticket-holders are by definition internet-active.
Flagship-event capture in a niche hobby typically runs 10–25% of the online-active
population: 13,000 ÷ 0.20 = 65,000; ÷ 0.10 = 130,000; ÷ 0.25 = 52,000. Instagram's 91K acts
as a soft ceiling.

**How Layer C was derived.** Bounded above by the 13,000 who pay for BreyerFest, multiplied
1.5–2.5× to capture serious collectors who don't travel to Kentucky. Bounded below by the
live-showing population — roughly 2,000–5,000 people in North America (187 shows × 20–40
entrants, heavily overlapping) — plus the 750–1,000 who reliably clear Web Special editions.

**Realistic platform trajectory:** a good free product might register **3,000–10,000 accounts
over two to three years**, with perhaps **100–500 paying** at typical niche conversion rates.

## 8. Two structural findings worth more than the headline numbers

**The hobby is bifurcating.** The casual/consumer end is healthy and growing — BreyerFest
went from ~1,000 to 30,000+ since 1990, and product releases are active. The **organised
competitive end is contracting**: NAN entrants down 45% and the event now biennial, IMEHA
dead since 2018, Blab still in recovery, the sanctioning body's own show database frozen at
2019. **A product weighted toward showing is aiming at the shrinking half of its own hobby.**

**Incumbent infrastructure is genuinely weak.** Ransomware, expired certificates, dead
organisations, a seven-year-old data cutoff at the governing body. That is real opportunity —
and it is opportunity in a market whose serious core is plausibly **20,000 people, not
200,000.**

The second finding is the counterweight to the first: the showing segment's decline is at
least partly *infrastructure failure* rather than lost interest, and since IMEHA closed in
2018 there has been no online-showing incumbent at all. That is the vacuum MHH is built for.
But the *volume* has to come from the collecting end, which is the half that is growing.
